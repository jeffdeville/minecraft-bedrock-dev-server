# Review of lessons 1 to 5

Reviewed 2026-09-07 against `course/lessons/01-manifest.md` … `05-event.md`,
`course/FORMAT.md`, the plan, the research report, `docs/learning-track.md`
("How a session runs", "Lesson 0"), the feasibility brief, `course/solutions/`,
the checker (`course/bin/lesson`) and the panel (`extension/media/main.js`,
`extension/src/extension.ts`).

Method: a scratch workspace from `course/bin/new-workspace`, then every step of
every lesson broken the way a twelve-year-old breaks it (wrong folder,
capitals, `.txt`, a comma in the wrong place, pasting the ```` ```json ````
fence, pasting the UUID over the quotes, typing the message with an apostrophe,
pressing "Write the answer" after using their own name and UUIDs), reading what
`lesson check`, `lesson hint`, `lesson answer` and `lesson json` (what the panel
renders) say for each. About 130 breakages in all. Every quoted message below
is the checker's real output. Nothing under the repository was changed except
this file.

---

## 1. Verdict

The lessons are competent, careful, technically right about Bedrock, and
dull in exactly the way that will lose this learner: three of the five
(1, 2 and 4) end with the sentence "nothing changes in the game", the course
is 60% manifest plumbing and UUID pasting before the kid sees a single line of
their own code do anything, and the text never once asks them to predict,
explain or vary anything, which is the whole method the parent uses in
`learning-track.md` (predict, make, play, teach-back). The prose is the right
register but there is too much of it for a 300-pixel side panel (lesson 1 is
372 words plus a 17-line code block whose lines scroll sideways), and the
Idea sections describe the file format rather than give the kid a reason to
care. Under the text, the checker has real holes that will produce the one
thing the plan says must not happen, a silent "no": JavaScript checks are
case-insensitive so `playerspawn` and `sendmessage` pass and die on the
server, a `.js` file pasted with its ```` ``` ```` fence passes `node --check`
as three template literals, a message with an apostrophe (`"Don't die!"`)
fails lesson 5, and "Write the answer" in lessons 2 and 4 replaces the
learner's own pack name and UUIDs with the solution's and breaks the BP→RP
link it was asked to fix. So: the medium is half the problem (panel, autosave,
deploy-restart-rejoin on a phone), the checks are a quarter, and the shape of
the sequence (no payoff until lesson 3, none again until 5, seven UUID
pastes) is the rest; the sentences themselves are the smallest part.

## 2. Cross-cutting problems, ranked by how many learners each stops

### 2.1 Three of five lessons end with nothing in the game

**What the kid does:** finishes lesson 1, reads "It does nothing yet. That
changes in the next lesson", finishes lesson 2, reads "Nothing looks different
yet, because the pack is still empty", does lesson 4, reads "Nothing changes in
the game, but the server has now loaded a script of yours". A kid motivated by
seeing something happen has now been told three times, in a course of five,
that nothing happened. The plan promised "every lesson ending with something
visible in game"; the research table marks lessons 1, 2 and 4 as **G**
(in-game milestone) and the authored lessons dropped all three to a `deploy`
check.

**The sentences:** 01 step 5 "It does nothing yet."; 02 step 5 "Nothing looks
different yet"; 04 step 5 "Nothing changes in the game".

**Fix, in order of cheapness:**

1. Lesson 1 and 2: give the kid somewhere to *see the name they chose*. On
   the phone, in the joined world: Pause → Settings → Behavior Packs / Resource
   Packs lists the server's active packs by name (and the icon, after lesson
   2). The parent must verify this screen on the Android client against the
   dedicated server before it goes in the text (it is a client screen that
   exists for worlds; confirm it is populated for a server world). If it is
   not, fall back to the deploy list only and shorten lessons 1 and 2 to ten
   minutes each, said plainly at the top: "This one is setup. It is short."
2. Lesson 4: make the deploy report whether the script loaded. The platform
   already runs with `CONTENT_LOG_CONSOLE_OUTPUT_ENABLED=true`; after the
   restart the sidecar can grep the container log between `Server started`
   and 10 s later for `[Scripting][error]` and write
   `"script_errors": ["..."]` into `deploy.json`. Then lesson 4 step 5's check
   is `{"deploy": "ok", "behavior_packs": ["kid_bp"], "scripts_ok": true}`
   (one new optional key on the `deploy` check, passing when
   `script_errors` is empty) and the text can say "the server says: script
   loaded, no errors", which is a real payoff for a setup lesson and the only
   way the kid ever learns the server *rejected* a script rather than ignored
   it. This is also what turns the honest but deflating sentence in 04 step 5
   ("If it had refused it, the deploy would still say live") into a feature.
3. Better still, merge 4 and 5: lesson 4's five steps are the setup for
   lesson 5's two. As one eight-step lesson ("Make the server say hello") the
   kid gets the first script payoff in the same session that did the plumbing
   (about 45 minutes, which is one session). The ids are fixed by FORMAT.md;
   if renumbering is off the table, put the sentence "Do lesson 5 today too:
   it is the payoff for this one" at the top of lesson 4's Idea.

### 2.2 A syntax error anywhere sends the panel back to step 1

**What the kid does:** on lesson 4 step 3, deletes the comma between the two
dependencies. The panel flips to **Step 1: Add a script module**, steps 2 to
4 lose their ticks, and the step body shown is step 1's ("Open
manifest.json. Inside the modules list… add a second module"), with the
message "a comma is missing at the end of the line before line 29". A kid
who reads the step body before the message (they do) starts re-adding a
module they already have, or pastes the starter text over their work. In
lesson 5, typing `world.sendMessage("Hel` and pausing a second (autosave)
does the same: the file is briefly invalid JS, step 1 fails, and its hint
("Every bracket opens and closes…") auto-opens on the second pause.

Verified with `lesson json`: `current_step: 1`, statuses
`[(1,current),(2,todo),(3,todo),(4,todo),(5,todo)]`. The terminal checker has
a guard ("Step 1 passed before, so something in the file stopped working.
`lesson answer` shows the fix.") but `lesson json` does not carry it and
`main.js` has nothing to render it.

**Fix (platform):** when the failing step is below `high` and the failing
check is a parse error (`msg` starts with the file name and contains "is not
valid JSON" or the `js` check failed `node --check`), `lesson json` should
report `current_step = high + 1`, keep steps ≤ high marked `done`, and add
`"broken": {"file": …, "msg": …}` to the lesson; the panel renders a single
red box "Your file stopped parsing: <msg>. Fix that line, or press Show the
answer" *above* the current step, and does not count the attempt toward the
hint. Also stop counting an attempt for saves that arrive within 3 seconds of
the previous one (typing), or debounce the attempt counter to the sidecar's
2-second quiet rule.

### 2.3 Checks that say yes to code the server will refuse (silent "no" in the game)

These are the cases the plan said the checker exists to prevent ("the game
says nothing"). Each passed `lesson check` in the scratch workspace.

| Lesson/step | Passes the check | What happens on the server | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 04 step 4, 05 both steps | `main.js` pasted with the ```` ```js ```` fence lines | Script does nothing, no error: the whole file is three template literals (`` ` `` `` ` `` `js…` `` ` `` `` ` ``), `node --check` accepts it, and the import regex still finds the text inside the string | `js` check has no "line starts with ```" rule |
| 05 step 1 | `world.afterEvents.playerspawn`, `world.afterevents.playerSpawn`, `World.afterEvents…` | `TypeError: cannot read 'subscribe' of undefined` at load; the pack's script is dead | `regex` is case-insensitive unless `exact: true`, and no lesson sets it |
| 05 step 2 | `world.sendmessage(…)`, `world.SendMessage(…)` | `TypeError` the first time a player spawns; nothing in chat | same |
| 04 step 1 | `"entry": "scripts/Main.js"` | BDS on Linux cannot find `Main.js`; script silently absent. This is pitfall 11 in the research report | `equals` is case-insensitive |
| 04 step 1 / 3 | `"language": "JavaScript"`, `"module_name": "@minecraft/Server"` | Unverified whether BDS accepts either; treat as silent failure until tested | same |
| 03 step 1 | `item.Diamond.name=…` | Nothing renamed | `regex` case-insensitive |
| 03 step 2 | `["en_us"]` | Client looks for `en_us.lang`, finds nothing; nothing renamed | `equals` case-insensitive |
| 05 step 1 | The import line deleted (select-all, paste over it) | `ReferenceError: world is not defined`; script dead | lesson 5 never re-checks lesson 4's import |

| 01 step 1 (laptop only) | Folder named `Kid_BP` | On the Linux box the folder is not found and the message has no "found Kid_BP instead" hint, because near-name detection only covers the file, not the folders | `check_exists` |

**Fix:** (a) `exact: true` on every `regex`, `equals`, `matches` that names a
JavaScript identifier, a file path, a language code or a module name: the
rule in the plan ("tolerant unless the lesson is about case") is wrong for
JS, where everything is about case. Better, make the checker treat `js`
files and any value under `entry`, `language`, `module_name` as exact by
default. (b) `js` check: fail on a line that starts with ```` ``` ```` with
the message "the first line is ```js — that is part of the lesson page, not
code; delete that line and the ``` at the end". (c) Lesson 5 step 1 repeats
lesson 4's import regex. (d) A cross-file uniqueness check: either extend
`differsFrom` to accept a `{"file": …, "path": …}` spec or add
`"differs": [SPEC, SPEC]` as the negation of `same`; lesson 2 step 2 then
asserts `kid_rp` header ≠ `kid_bp` header and ≠ `kid_bp` module. (e)
`check_exists` walks each path component looking for a case-insensitive
near-match and says which folder is misspelled.

### 2.4 "Write the answer" undoes the learner's own work

**What the kid does:** named their pack "Dragon Pack" in lesson 1 with their
own UUIDs, is stuck on lesson 2 step 4 (the dependency), presses **Show the
answer**. The diff offers to change `"Dragon Pack"` to `"Wizard Pack"`, both
UUIDs to the solution's, and the dependency to the solution's RP UUID. They
press **Write the answer into my files**. Result, verified: the very same
step still fails ("kid_bp says 8e4d2f6a… but kid_rp says 22222222…") because
the "answer" pointed at a resource pack they do not have. On lesson 4 step 1
the same button silently rewrites the header UUID, module UUID, name and RP
dependency of a file that was fine, and the lesson-4 checks do not notice
because they only look at `modules[1]` and `dependencies[1]`; the pack now
depends on a UUID that exists nowhere and the name the kid chose is gone.

`keep_learner_values` only protects paths that have a `json` check *in the
current lesson*; the RP dependency has a `same` check, not a `json` check, and
lesson 4 has no checks on lesson-1 paths at all.

**Fix (platform):** the answer must be rendered against the learner's
identifiers, not the solution's. Simplest robust version: at
`new-workspace` time generate the workspace's UUIDs once into
`.course/ids.json` (`bp_header`, `bp_module`, `rp_header`, `rp_module`,
`script_module`) and make `build-tags` render `{{BP_HEADER}}`-style
placeholders in `course/solutions/**` from that file before committing the
tags, so the solution tags *are* the learner's UUIDs. Then extend
`keep_learner_values` to preserve `header.name` and `header.description`
across every lesson (a fixed list of "learner-owned" paths in the checker),
and make `lesson answer` refuse to touch a file the current lesson's checks
do not fail on. Until that lands, the "Write the answer" button in lessons 2
and 4 should be hidden (`files` in the frontmatter can list only the file the
step creates, but that does not help for step 4 of lesson 2, which edits
`kid_bp/manifest.json`).

### 2.5 Reading load in a 300-pixel panel

Measured: lesson 1 is 135 words of Idea plus 237 words of step prose plus a
17-line code block; lesson 2 is 329 words, 4 is 316, 5 is 253. Hints run 27
to 64 words. The longest code lines are 44 characters (lessons 1 and 2) and
52 characters (lesson 5, `world.afterEvents.playerSpawn.subscribe((event)
=> {`), which at the panel's 0.92 em monospace scroll sideways in the
default sidebar width, so the kid sees `world.afterEvents.playerSpawn.subsc`
and a scrollbar. The Idea is collapsed from step 2 on, which is right, but
the current step body plus the failing message plus the auto-opened hint plus
the answer diff can stack to two screens.

**What the kid does:** reads the first two lines of the step, finds the code
block, copies it, stops reading. Everything after the code block (01 step 1
"Do not worry about what it all means yet"; 05 step 1 "Read it right to left:
subscribe a function…"; 04 step 3 "Two things are different about this entry
on purpose") is never read. Sentences that matter after a code block are
wasted; sentences that matter *before* it get skimmed past because the code
block is the visible target.

**Fix (text):** hard limits per step: ≤ 40 words before the code block, the
code block, ≤ 20 words after (and only if it is an instruction, never an
explanation). Move every explanation ("that word tells Minecraft…") into the
check message or the Idea. Idea ≤ 100 words, two paragraphs, second one is
the prediction prompt. Break long code lines: `world.afterEvents.playerSpawn`
then `.subscribe((event) => {` on the next line is valid JS and fits. Hints ≤
30 words: one thing to look at, one thing to do.

**Fix (platform):** the panel could render the code block with a **Copy**
button and the words "copy this" above it, so the kid's eye has a target and
the author has a place to put the one sentence that must be read before the
paste. The step list could show step titles as imperatives with the file
name ("Name your pack — manifest.json"), so the list itself is the map.

### 2.6 Nobody is asked to predict, explain or vary anything

The parent's session shape is: say back what the step is for, predict what
the change will do, make it, play it, check the prediction, teach back one
sentence. The lessons contain zero prediction prompts and zero teach-back
prompts. Latitude exists only where it is free (the pack name, the message
text, "try one more" in lesson 3). Every step is "paste this".

**What the kid does:** pastes, watches the tick, moves on, cannot say what a
manifest is at the end of lesson 1 because they were never asked. The
parent, following `learning-track.md`, asks at the end of the session and
gets a shrug; the parent concludes the lessons "are not very good", which is
the right conclusion for the wrong reason: the lessons *worked* as a
checklist and *failed* as teaching.

**Fix (text, cheap, no platform change):** every Idea ends with one bold
**Predict:** line ("where will the name you type show up?"); every payoff
step ends with "Was your prediction right?" and one **Say it back:** line
that is the teach-back question from the learning track ("what event starts
it, what does it change, what do you see?"). Every lesson gets one **Try
this** variation at the end that cannot break anything and has a visible
effect (lesson 3: rename a second item; lesson 5: change the message; lesson
1: rename `manifest.json` to `manifest.txt`, watch the pack vanish from the
status bar, rename it back — productive failure, and it is exactly the
learning track's 0b method). None of these need a check; the panel renders
them as text.

### 2.8 Seven UUID pastes

Lesson 1: two. Lesson 2: two, plus one copy. Lesson 4: one. Six `lesson uuid`
runs and one cross-file copy in the first four lessons; only the copy (the
dependency) teaches anything after the first paste. Each paste is a
select-the-right-characters exercise with three known failure modes (over the
quotes, after the placeholder, the wrong slot) and its own step, so the
course's step count is padded with mechanics.

**Fix:** keep exactly the pastes that teach: one in lesson 1 (both numbers in
one step, so the kid does the move twice and learns "every uuid line gets a
fresh one"), the dependency copy in lesson 2 (the idea of the lesson), one in
lesson 4 (new module, new number, they know the move). Lesson 2's two RP
UUIDs should be pre-filled: either the RP manifest ships in the template
with UUIDs generated per workspace (see 2.4, `.course/ids.json`), or lesson
2's starter text is written by a new `lesson new-pack kid_rp resources`
command that creates the folder and manifest with fresh UUIDs (small, stdlib,
and it also kills the "wrong folder" class of mistakes for lesson 2). Either
way the lesson text says "the numbers are already filled in; look at them,
they are different from kid_bp's", which is the point.

### 2.9 Rejoining the world on a phone

Every payoff step says "leave your world and join it again" as if the kid
were still in it. They are not: the deploy *restarts the server*
(`RELOAD_MODE` restart per B3), so the phone shows "Disconnected from
server" the moment the deploy lands, whether or not they were doing anything.
Rejoining on Android is Play → Servers tab → scroll → tap the server → Join
Server → (from lesson 2 on) a "This server wants to use resource packs"
prompt → Download → wait. That is five taps and 20 to 40 seconds, and the
resource pack prompt is a place to get it wrong: tapping "Join" instead of
"Download" or having declined once means lesson 3's diamond stays "Diamond"
and nothing says why.

Also: 03 step 3 says "hover over a diamond". There is no hover on a phone.

**Fix (text):** one shared paragraph, used verbatim in every payoff step: "The
bottom bar says **live**. Your phone says *Disconnected*: that was your server
restarting with your pack in it. Tap your server again. If it asks about
resource packs, tap **Download**." Lesson 3: "Put a diamond in your hotbar and
select it: its name shows above the hotbar." **Fix (platform):** set
`texturepack-required=true` on the learner servers so the client cannot join
without the pack; then the prompt has one button and lesson 3 cannot fail
silently. **Fix (parent):** make the world creative before lesson 3, or have
`/give` ready; the parent-ahead list in section 5 has this.

### 2.10 Autosave versus "Paste this in and save"

The template turns on `files.autoSave: afterDelay` (1 s). Lesson text says
"Paste this in and save", "Save.", "Save and wait". The kid looks for a save
button, or presses Ctrl-S and nothing visible happens. Worse, autosave
means every pause while typing is an attempt: the hint auto-opens after two
failed attempts, so a kid typing a name slowly gets a hint for a step they
are in the middle of doing. Combined with 2.2 the panel is *jumping* while
they type.

**Fix (text):** say once, in lesson 1 step 1: "The file saves itself a second
after you stop typing; you never press save." Never say "Save." again; say
"Stop typing and watch the bottom bar." **Fix (platform):** count an attempt
only when the failing check is different from the last one, or 5 seconds
after the last edit, and never auto-open a hint for a parse error.

### 2.11 Does each lesson teach one idea?

- **Lesson 1** teaches three: folder + manifest (the idea), UUIDs (a second
  idea with its own mechanics), and the save→deploy→live loop (never
  explained anywhere; it is the most important mental model in the course
  and the kid meets it as "Within about fifteen seconds your course page
  shows a new deploy"). Cut the second paragraph of the Idea (it is lesson
  2's idea: "Later there is a second half, the resource pack"). Give the
  loop its own picture (section 5).
- **Lesson 2** teaches two: the resource pack, and the *link* by UUID
  (`dependencies`), plus an icon step with no visible payoff. The link is the
  idea; the Idea section says so in the second paragraph. The icon should go
  wherever the pack list becomes visible (see 2.1) or be dropped.
- **Lesson 3** teaches one idea and is the best of the five: a real payoff, a
  clear analogy (a dictionary), latitude ("any name in the game"), and a
  variation. It only needs the phone fix and the case fixes.
- **Lesson 4** teaches nothing the kid can hold: the Idea admits it ("has to
  promise three things") and step 5 admits it ("Nothing changes"). It is
  setup. Merge with 5 or label it as setup and make the server's "script
  loaded" line the payoff.
- **Lesson 5** teaches one idea (an event runs code) and then dumps arrow
  function syntax into the Idea ("It is written as `(event) => { ... }`:
  the part in parentheses is what the server hands you"). Move that sentence
  to step 1's hint; the Idea should stay with the pressure plate.

### 2.12 Do the Idea sections teach or describe?

Lesson 1 describes the file ("It says the pack's name, what kind of pack it
is, and gives it two serial numbers") and the analogy is weak ("the way a
chest with no sign is just another chest": a chest without a sign is
perfectly findable). Lesson 2 describes ("Minecraft treats them as two
separate packs, each with its own ID card"). Lesson 3 teaches (the key, the
dictionary, "one line of text, no code"). Lesson 4 describes a checklist.
Lesson 5 teaches (pressure plate wired to a door) then describes syntax. A
teaching Idea has: one concrete situation the kid already knows, the new idea
as the name for that situation, and a question. Section 6 shows the shape.

### 2.13 Is "one edit per step" honored?

Mostly honored, and where it is faked the fake is the comma: 02 step 4 ("Mind
the comma: the `]` that closes `modules` needs a comma after it now") and 04
steps 1 and 3 ("Mind the comma between them") are one paste plus one comma,
and the comma is where every one of those steps fails in testing. Two
options: make the pasted snippet *start* with the comma (`,\n  "dependencies":
[`) and say "paste this right after the `]`", so the edit really is one
paste; or have step N end with the comma placed and step N+1 be the paste.
The first is better. Lesson 2 step 2 (two pastes) and lesson 1 steps 3 and 4
(two steps for the same move) are the other direction: two edits that are
one idea, and should be one step.

### 2.14 What the parent needs to know (and is not told anywhere)

There is no parent-facing page. The parent needs, per lesson: what the kid
will see when it works, the one screen on the phone to open, the two
teach-back questions, what to have ready (creative mode, a diamond, cheats on
for `/give`), and what a stuck kid looks like (the panel on step 1 with a
parse error means "a comma or quote", not "start over"). Section 5 gives one
line per lesson; they should live in `course/PARENT.md`, one paragraph per
lesson, rendered on the dashboard.

## 3. Lesson by lesson

Ratings: **good** = the message says what is wrong and what to do in the
kid's words; **vague** = true but the kid cannot act on it; **wrong** =
misleading, contradictory, or a false pass/fail.

### Lesson 1: A pack is a folder with a manifest

## 4. Measurement design

The point is to answer, after a week, "where does the kid stop, and what
did the panel say at that moment", without ever storing what the kid typed.

### Events

Appended by the checker (`lesson`) and by the extension, one JSON object per
line, to `<workspace>/.course/events.jsonl` (already gitignored via
`.course/`). The extension writes through the checker (`lesson event
<name> [key=value…]`, a new subcommand, so there is one writer and one
schema) rather than touching the file itself. The platform's sidecar already
bind-mounts the workspace; the control plane copies the file on its existing
progress poll and keeps it per learner. Rotation: the checker truncates to the
last 20 000 lines on start.

| Event | Emitted by | Fields beyond the common ones |
|---|---|---|
| `check` | every `lesson check`/`json` run | `attempt` (bool), `lesson`, `step` (first failing, or null), `passed`, `high`, `check` (label of the failing check), `kind` (json/regex/js/exists/same/deploy/game), `code` (see below), `line` (int or null), `regressed` (bool: failing step < high), `elapsed_ms` |
| `hint` | `lesson hint`, panel Hint button, auto-open | `lesson`, `step`, `auto` (bool) |
| `answer` | `lesson answer` | `lesson`, `step`, `applied` (bool), `files_changed` |
| `next` | `lesson next` | `from`, `to`, `ok` |
| `deploy` | checker, when `deploy.json`'s `at` changes | `status`, `code` (parse-error class from `detail`, or `duplicate_uuid`, `other`), `duration_seconds`, `behavior_packs`, `resource_packs`, `script_errors` (count) |
| `game` | game checks | `lesson`, `pass` (bool), `code` (grader reason, first 40 chars, it is authored text), `elapsed_ms` |
| `panel` | extension | `action`: `open`, `check_click`, `hint_click`, `answer_click`, `apply_click`, `next_click`, `game_click`, `open_file`, `open_lesson`, `idea_toggle`, `seen_it` (a new "I saw it in the game" button on payoff steps) |
| `session` | extension | `action`: `start` (panel first render), `end` (window blur > 10 min or close), `active_ms` |

`code` for check failures is an enum, never the diagnostic text: `missing_file`,
`near_name`, `json_empty`, `json_fence`, `json_missing_comma`,
`json_extra_comma`, `json_unterminated_string`, `json_truncated`,
`json_no_quotes`, `json_extra_data`, `json_bom`, `json_comments`,
`path_missing`, `value_unchanged`, `value_wrong`, `value_empty`, `uuid_placeholder`,
`uuid_malformed`, `uuid_duplicate`, `uuid_cross_file`, `regex_missing`,
`regex_count`, `js_syntax`, `js_fence`, `js_console_log`, `js_before_events`,
`js_world_top_level`, `same_mismatch`, `deploy_failed`, `deploy_missing_pack`,
`deploy_waiting`, `game_fail`, `checker_broken`. `explain_json_error` already
branches on exactly these cases; it returns the code alongside the sentence.

### One event

```json
{"t":"2026-09-07T14:02:11Z","learner":"alice","v":1,"e":"check","attempt":true,"lesson":"05-event","step":2,"passed":1,"high":1,"check":"packs/kid_bp/scripts/main.js contains world.sendMessage","kind":"regex","code":"regex_missing","line":null,"regressed":false,"elapsed_ms":180}
```

`learner` is the name the platform already holds (the workspace directory
name); `v` is the schema version.

### Never logged

- Any string the child typed: the pack name, the description, the message
  in `sendMessage`, the lang value, file contents, the `got` text (which today
  contains `header.name is still "Dragon Pack"`). Only the `code` and the
  line number go in. If a diagnostic is needed for debugging a check, log
  the *check's expected value* (authored text), never the found one.
- Terminal input, key presses, clipboard.
- Anything from `lessons/` edits (the kid may open the lesson source).
- IP, device, times of day finer than the timestamp on the event itself
  (which is needed for session length; keep it, it is not shared).

### Five questions and their queries

1. **Which step takes the most attempts before it passes?** Group `check`
   events with `attempt=true` by `(lesson, step)` and count rows until the
   first event where `passed >= step`; report the median and max per step.
2. **Which failure code is most common per step, and does its message get
   fixed?** Group `check` failures by `(lesson, step, code)`; for each, the
   mean number of further attempts on the same step before passing (a high
   number means the message did not help).
3. **Where do sessions end?** For each `session.end`, take the last `check`
   event before it: histogram of `(lesson, step, code)`; a step that ends
   sessions while failing is the first thing to rewrite.
4. **Which hints are opened and do they help?** Count `hint` per `(lesson,
   step)` split by `auto`; then attempts-to-pass after the hint versus
   without it; and `answer` with `applied=true` per step, which is the
   "gave up" signal.
5. **How long from a correct save to the kid seeing it?** For each payoff
   step, time from the `check` that passed the file checks to the `deploy`
   event with `status=ok`, and from that to `panel.seen_it`; long gaps with
   no `seen_it` are the rejoin problem, not a lesson problem.

Two more that fall out for free: attempts that are `regressed=true` (the
panel jumped back, 2.2) per lesson; and the `deploy` codes over a week, which
tell the author what the static checks missed.

# A pack is a folder with an ID card

## The idea

Minecraft finds a pack the way a courier finds a house: by the address. The
address is a folder inside `packs`, and inside that folder one file named
exactly `manifest.json`. That file is the pack's ID card: the name you give
it, plus two serial numbers (UUIDs) that no other pack anywhere shares. If the
card is missing or its name is spelled wrong, Minecraft walks past without a
word.

Today you make the ID card and watch your server read it.

**Say it out loud before you start:** when the server reads your card, where
do you think your pack's name will show up?

## Step 1: Make the folder and the file
<!-- check
{"exists": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "msg": "I cannot find packs/kid_bp/manifest.json. The folder is kid_bp and the file is manifest.json, all lowercase, ending in .json (not .txt)"}
{"json": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "path": "header", "exists": true, "msg": "the file does not have the whole starter text yet. Select all, delete, and paste the starter text again, from the first { to the last }"}
{"json": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "path": "modules[0]", "exists": true, "msg": "the modules part at the bottom is missing. Select all, delete, and paste the starter text again, all the way to the last }"}
-->
In the file tree on the left: right-click `packs` → **New Folder** → type
`kid_bp`. Then right-click `kid_bp` → **New File** → type `manifest.json`.

Paste this into the empty file. Copy from the first `{` to the last `}`
only, not the ``` lines:

```json
{
  "format_version": 2,
  "header": {
    "name": "My Pack",
    "description": "My first behavior pack",
    "uuid": "PASTE-A-UUID-HERE",
    "version": [1, 0, 0],
    "min_engine_version": [1, 26, 0]
  },
  "modules": [
    {
      "type": "data",
      "uuid": "PASTE-A-UUID-HERE",
      "version": [1, 0, 0]
    }
  ]
}
```

The file saves itself a second after you stop typing; you never need to press
save. Leave the two `PASTE-A-UUID-HERE` lines alone for now: they are step 3.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
Look at the file tree: it should show `packs` → `kid_bp` → `manifest.json`.
If the file is somewhere else, drag it into `kid_bp`. If its name is wrong,
right-click it → **Rename**. If the editor shows a red squiggle, the paste
is incomplete: select all, delete, paste again.
</details>

## Step 2: Name your pack
<!-- check
{"json": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "path": "header.name", "not": "My Pack", "exact": true, "msg": "the name still says My Pack. On the line \"name\": \"My Pack\", change only the words My Pack. Keep the quotes and the comma"}
{"json": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "path": "header.name", "matches": "\\S", "msg": "the name is empty. Put a name between the quotes after \"name\":"}
-->
Find the line `"name": "My Pack"`. Change the words `My Pack` to a name you
like. Keep the quotes around it and the comma after it: the quotes tell
Minecraft where the name starts and ends.

**Predict:** where will this name appear? You find out in step 4.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
Only the words between the second pair of quotes change. The line should
still look like `"name": "Something You Chose",` and the line above it,
`"header": {`, stays as it is.
</details>

## Step 3: Give it its two serial numbers
<!-- check
{"json": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "path": "header.uuid", "uuid": true, "msg": "the first PASTE-A-UUID-HERE (in the header part, near the top) is not a serial number yet. In the terminal type: lesson uuid  then copy the answer, select the words PASTE-A-UUID-HERE, and paste"}
{"json": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "path": "modules[0].uuid", "uuid": true, "msg": "the second PASTE-A-UUID-HERE (in the modules part, near the bottom) is not a serial number yet. Type lesson uuid again for a new one and paste it there"}
{"json": "packs/kid_bp/manifest.json", "path": "modules[0].uuid", "differsFrom": "header.uuid", "msg": "both serial numbers are the same. Type lesson uuid once more and paste the new one over the second uuid, in the modules part"}
-->
A UUID is a serial number so long that no two in the world are alike. The
terminal is the black box at the bottom of the editor. Click in it, type
`lesson uuid`, press Enter, and it prints one, like
`9f2c1a7e-3b4d-4c5e-8a6f-1d2e3f4a5b6c`.

Copy it. In the file, select the words `PASTE-A-UUID-HERE` (only the words,
not the quotes) and paste. Then do it again for the second one, further down:
type `lesson uuid` again, because the two must be different.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
Double-click on `PASTE-A-UUID-HERE` selects part of it; drag across the whole
thing instead. The line should end up as `"uuid": "9f2c...-...-...-...-...",`
with the quotes still there. If `lesson uuid` prints nothing, click inside the
terminal first.
</details>

## Step 4: See the server read it
<!-- check
{"deploy": "ok", "behavior_packs": ["kid_bp"], "msg": "the server has not installed kid_bp yet. Look at the bar at the bottom of the editor: if it says deploy failed, click it to read why; if it is still working, give it a few seconds"}
-->
Look at the bar at the bottom of the editor. Within about fifteen seconds it
says **live: kid_bp**. That is the whole trip: the file saved, your server
found the folder, read the ID card, and now knows your pack.

On your phone, join your server. Pause → **Settings** → **Behavior Packs**.
Your pack is in the list, with the name you chose. Was your prediction right?

**Try this:** rename `manifest.json` to `manifest.txt` and watch the bottom
bar. Your pack disappears without a word. Rename it back.

**Say it back to someone:** what is a manifest, and what happens if its name
is spelled wrong?

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
If the bottom bar says **deploy failed**, click it: the message names the
problem, usually a missing comma or quote. Fix it and the server tries again
by itself. If the phone shows "Disconnected", that was the server restarting
with your pack: tap your server again to join.
</details>
````

Two platform notes the rewrite depends on, both small: `explain_json_error`
should recognise a bare UUID where a string was expected (2.7), and the
`exists` diagnostic should search `packs/**` for the basename (2.7). Without
them the rewrite's step 1 and step 3 messages are still better than today's,
but the two most common breakages (`.txt`, pasted over the quotes) still get
a wrong diagnosis.
